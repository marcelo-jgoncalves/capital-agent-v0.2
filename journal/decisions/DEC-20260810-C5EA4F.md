## Opportunity

A companion/checklist digital product (guide, not software service) helping
individual MEIs (Microempreendedores Individuais) understand and stay
compliant with the new NFS-e Nacional regime — plain-language, aimed at
non-technical solo workers, strictly organizational/educational, never
filing anything on the user's behalf and never issuing definitive tax
classification.

## Mechanism of return

Sold as a low-cost digital download (guide + checklist + deadline
reminders) on a Brazilian infoproduct platform (Hotmart/Kiwify — Kiwify
identified earlier as cheapest for starting volume), monetizing genuine,
timely confusion around a real regulatory change, without building or
operating a SaaS service.

## Evidence

- `WebSearch`, 2026-08-10: NFS-e Nacional (via gov.br) has been mandatory
  for MEIs providing services since 2023, with full national-standard
  migration for municipalities over 50,000 inhabitants expected in the
  second half of 2026 — meaning confusion is current and ongoing, not a
  one-time event already absorbed by the market. MEI billing limit for
  2026 is BRL 81,000/year (BRL 6,750/month), unchanged since 2019 — a
  concrete, checkable fact a guide would need to state correctly.
- Real stakes: an MEI who fails to issue a required invoice risks having
  their CNPJ cancelled and a fine of up to 75% of the operation's value —
  found via a second, deeper search after an initial (looser, "5%") figure
  from the first pass turned out to refer to a narrower case (wrong
  document type). Always re-verify the most severe stated consequence
  before scoping a product around a regulatory topic — the first number
  found is not automatically the right one to design around.
- Competitive check (`WebFetch` on `blog.vimbo.com.br`'s own comparison
  post): existing commercial tools (Vimbo and similar) target "gestores de
  todos os portes" (managers of all sizes) with business-integration
  features (banking reconciliation, cash flow, multi-user) — B2B-shaped
  tooling. The same source states public/official emitters "lack
  user-friendly interfaces and support," calling this out as a gap for
  "less-technical autonomous workers" specifically. No product found
  targeting individual, non-technical MEIs with a simple, plain-language
  companion guide.
- Contrast with the three ideas killed earlier this cycle
  (`context/knowledge/rejected-opportunities.md`): those were all sourced
  from generic "best ideas" search and were, unsurprisingly, already
  crowded. This one was sourced from a live, dated regulatory event
  (`2026-01-01` new obligations for autonomous workers) — closer to the
  corrected research method logged in
  `context/knowledge/recurring-errors.md`.

## Why this could fail

- **Legal/liability risk is real and was already flagged to the human
  owner**, who explicitly authorized continuing "com cautela" (with
  caution) — see below for how scope is constrained to manage this.
- Rules and thresholds change; a guide can go stale and give
  outdated/wrong information if not maintained. Mitigated by scope (a
  guide with a clear "verified as of [date], rules can change, confirm on
  gov.br or with an accountant" framing) and by treating this as a small,
  cheap first product, not a large one-time investment.
- Demand is inferred from search evidence (confusion exists, gap in
  tooling exists) but not yet directly validated with real buyers — this
  decision proposes further scoping and a cheap validation step, not an
  immediate full build.
- Competitive landscape could shift quickly given how topical the
  regulatory change is; other creators may already be producing similar
  guides that didn't surface in this search pass.

## Alternatives considered

- **A full SaaS tool** (like Vimbo) that actually files/manages NFS-e:
  rejected for this first product — far higher build effort, and directly
  touches the "new counterparty/platform with write authority" and
  execution-adjacent risk categories this project's own custody-invariant
  design philosophy exists to avoid taking on lightly. A pure information
  product carries much less liability than a tool that files anything.
- **Do nothing / keep searching**: rejected as the sole path — this is the
  first candidate in three cycles with real, current, non-crowded signal
  behind it; worth a cheap next step rather than discarding on caution
  alone.

## Scope constraint (how liability is managed)

The product must, by design:
- Never file, submit, or transmit anything to any government system on the
  buyer's behalf (no execution capability — same principle as this
  project's own custody invariant, applied here to tax filings instead of
  money movement).
- Never assert a definitive tax/legal classification for a reader's
  specific situation.
- State prominently, near the top, that it is educational/organizational,
  not accounting or legal advice, and direct genuinely uncertain cases to
  an accountant or the official gov.br channels.
- Cite its sources and the date verified, so staleness is visible rather
  than silently wrong.

## Expected-value view

- Downside: near-zero capital (a guide requires writing/formatting effort,
  not software infrastructure; platform listing fees are percentage-based,
  charged only on actual sales).
- Base case: a real, sellable digital product exists and can be tested
  against genuine demand via marketplace listing, at low labor cost to the
  human owner (creation effort falls to the Capital Agent; the human owner's
  role is limited to account creation on the chosen platform and reviewing
  the final content before it goes public, per the identity/reputation
  critical-decision gate).
- Upside: if this specific guide validates, the same low-labor-content
  pattern (identify a live, narrow, non-crowded regulatory/informational
  gap; produce a careful, scoped, non-advice guide; sell cheaply) could
  repeat for other topics without needing a fundamentally new business
  model each time.
- Confidence: medium — real evidence behind the gap and the timing, but no
  direct demand validation (a pre-sale or landing-page signal test) has
  been run yet.

## Decision

**Observe → prepare.** This is the first growth-vector candidate in this
project's search that survived initial research on both demand-gap and
competitive-crowding grounds. Next step: draft the guide's outline and
content (research/preparation, no gate required), verify every factual
claim (thresholds, deadlines, penalties) directly against the official
gov.br/Receita Federal sources rather than secondary blog posts, and design
a cheap validation step (e.g. a simple landing page describing the guide,
testing whether anyone signals real interest, before finalizing and
publishing it for sale) before treating this as fully validated.

## Kill condition

Kill if: direct verification against primary government sources turns up
materially different facts than what secondary sources reported here;
or a validation/landing-page test shows no real interest; or the human
owner's review of the drafted content surfaces a legal/reputational concern
outweighing the opportunity.

## Addendum (2026-08-10T16:30:00-03:00) — Primary-source verification in progress, real complexity found

First primary-source check (`WebFetch` on `gov.br/nfse`'s own news page)
confirmed the topic has more regulatory nuance than the secondary sources
captured: there are **two distinct, easy-to-conflate rules**, not one:

1. MEI's own NFS-e obligation, in force since 2023 (Resolução CGSN 169/22),
   applying specifically to services rendered to a legal entity (CNPJ)
   client — not to every transaction.
2. A separate, newer migration requiring ME/EPP optantes do Simples
   Nacional to use the National Emitter starting `01/09/2026` (Resolução
   189/2026) — a different taxpayer category (small companies, not
   individual MEIs) and a different deadline than item 1.

A guide that conflated these two rules, or applied the 2026 ME/EPP deadline
to MEI's already-standing 2023 obligation (or vice versa), would be exactly
the kind of error this decision's "Scope constraint" section was written to
prevent. This is not yet fully resolved — more primary-source verification
is needed (confirm MEI-specific rules precisely, confirm whether MEI is
also swept into the `01/09/2026` migration or stays under its 2023 rule,
confirm current penalty text with exact source) before any content is
drafted. Flagged as real, in-progress complexity rather than treated as
already resolved.

## Addendum 2 (2026-08-10T16:40:00-03:00) — MEI-specific rule confirmed against the official portal

Further verification, this time directly against `gov.br/nfse`'s own pages
(via `WebSearch` results quoting official page titles/content, e.g. "MEI
prestadores de Serviço de Todo o País Estão Obrigados a Emitir NFS-e" and
"Resolução CGSN nº 169 traz novos benefícios ao MEI") and cross-checked
against a second independent legislative-text host (LegisWeb), converged
on a consistent, specific picture for the MEI rule (Resolução CGSN nº 169,
27/07/2022):

- Applies only to MEI **service providers** — does not apply to MEI selling
  merchandise.
- Mandatory NFS-e issuance only when the client is a **legal entity (CNPJ)**
  — issuance for individual (CPF) clients remains **optional**.
- MEI is prohibited from issuing NFS-e for operations subject solely to
  ICMS (a goods-tax regime, out of scope for the service-only rule above).
- In force since **01/09/2023** — already standing law, not the newer
  `01/09/2026` deadline, which applies to the separate ME/EPP National
  Emitter migration (Resolução 189/2026) and does not appear to change
  MEI's own, already-established 2023 requirement.

**Not yet confirmed against a primary source**: the exact penalty for
non-compliance (secondary sources cited figures ranging from 5% for the
wrong document type to 75% / CNPJ cancellation for non-issuance
altogether, and these were not re-verified against the Estatuto do MEI or
CGSN resolution text directly). The guide will state that penalties exist
and can be severe, cite that this specific figure needs confirmation
against the primary legal text or an accountant, and will not assert an
unverified number as fact. This is consistent with the decision's scope
constraint — organize and explain, never assert unverified specifics with
real financial consequences attached.

Sufficient confidence now exists in the core scope/timing facts to proceed
to drafting an outline (not final content) for human review, per the
original next-step plan.
